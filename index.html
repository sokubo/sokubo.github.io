Shoki Okubo

I am an Assistant Professor of Sociology at Toyo University, Tokyo. I work on causal inference under model uncertainty: which covariates a study should adjust for, how robustness should be reported honestly, and what data-driven variable selection does to statistical inference.

My methodological work sits between sociological methodology and statistics. Current projects develop causally disciplined multiverse analysis — running robustness analyses only within specifications that a stated causal model licenses, and decomposing disagreement across specifications into sampling noise and structural uncertainty — together with valid post-selection inference for outcome-adaptive covariate selection and estimand-specific theory of optimal adjustment sets.

Substantively I study care work, long-term care policy, and inequality over the life course, mostly with large-scale Japanese survey and panel data. I am a member of the Japanese Life Course Panel Surveys (JLPS) team at the Institute of Social Science, The University of Tokyo, where I was on the faculty from 2018 to 2025. I hold a PhD in Human Sciences from Osaka University (2017).

Research interests: sociological methodology · statistical models for social data · computational social science · social policy · social stratification · institutional change.
